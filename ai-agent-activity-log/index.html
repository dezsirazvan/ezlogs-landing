Facts first, AI optional

Every card is built from the events your app sent, by fixed rules: the same events always give the same card, and nothing is invented. AI only sits on top, clearly labelled: an explanation of one action, a short story of an agent’s day, and your own AI asking questions.

It watches. It never acts.

EZLogs is read-only by design. It never writes back into your app: no pause buttons, no approval queues, no MCP tools that make anything happen. Phone numbers, addresses, birth dates, IPs and document numbers are never stored, passwords and tokens are masked inside your app before anything is sent, and data is hosted in Finland (EU).

How it differs from agent tracing tools

LangSmith, Langfuse and Braintrust show an engineer the agent’s prompts, tokens and traces. EZLogs shows the whole company what the agent did to your business: which bookings it changed, which refunds failed, and why. Many teams use both.

Questions

How does EZLogs know an action was done by an AI agent?

From who made the request: calls from Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor or Windsurf are recognised from the client name they send, and your own assistant shows as an agent when your app tells EZLogs who is acting. A person or the system is labelled as such. EZLogs never guesses: an actor it cannot tell stays unnamed.

Does AI write the activity log?

No. Every card is built from the events your app sent, by fixed rules, so the same events always give the same card. AI is optional on top: a labelled explanation or summary, and your own AI asking questions over MCP.

Can EZLogs stop or change what an agent does?

No. EZLogs is read-only by design: it never writes back into your app, and its MCP tools only answer questions. It tells your team what happened and alerts them when something fails.

How is this different from LangSmith, Langfuse or Braintrust?

Those tools show an engineer the agent’s prompts, tokens and traces. EZLogs shows the whole team what the agent did to your business: which bookings it changed, which refunds failed and why. Many teams use both.
